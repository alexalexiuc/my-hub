---

## Summary

The Calorie Tracker MCP server lets an AI client log meals, retrieve nutritional
summaries, manage the user's dietary profile, and track body measurements over time
on behalf of the authenticated user. Implemented as a Fastify module backed by
PostgreSQL on the self-hosted platform.

| FR-42 | Weight-goal progress is **cumulative**, not weekly. The projection runs from a stored baseline on `calorie_profiles` (`goal_start_date`, `goal_start_weight_kg`) rather than re-anchoring to each week's first weigh-in. The old behaviour silently forgave every missed week — a week that ended 1 kg over target became the next week's starting point, so two weeks of a 1 kg/week goal could still target only 1 kg down, and a month of misses still read "on track". The baseline is stamped by `upsertCalorieProfile` when a goal is first set and when `goalType`/`goalWeeklyRateKg` change, and otherwise moves only through the Progress card's explicit "Restart from today" action (or an explicit `goalStartDate` + `goalStartWeightKg` on `calories_update_profile`, which must be passed together). Profiles with a goal but no baseline are backfilled from their oldest weigh-in within the preceding 8 weeks (migration `0021`); anything still unanchored falls back to the oldest weigh-in on hand, and the card labels that baseline as estimated.                                                                       |
| FR-43 | The goal is judged on a **smoothed trend weight**, not the latest scale reading. Day-to-day weight swings 1–1.5 kg on water and glycogen alone, which is larger than the ~1 kg/week a goal moves, so a single reading compares mostly noise — one hard weekend used to flip the card between "ahead" and "behind". The Progress card and the weight-trend chart both plot the raw weigh-in faintly beneath an exponentially-weighted trend line (α = 0.1, applied **per day elapsed** rather than per reading — stepping once per reading made the smoothing depend on how often someone stood on the scale, so a month with nothing logged advanced the average by a single step and the line coming out of the gap still mostly described the weight going into it; readings sharing a date are averaged into one value first), and every headline figure (current weight vs target, ahead/behind, total change) reads from the trend. The card also states the rate actually being achieved (least-squares slope of the trend, kg/week) against the goal rate, which is the figure that says whether the deficit needs changing.           |
| FR-44 | Both weight charts have a selectable window: the goal progress card offers 4W/8W/12W/All (4W default — over 7 days the goal signal is smaller than the noise, over 4 weeks it is clearly ahead of it) and the weight trend chart offers 4W/8W/12W/6M/1Y/All (12W default). The trend chart previously showed a fixed "last 20 weigh-ins", which is a count rather than a period and was silently capped by the API's `limit`. Ranges are applied client-side over one full-history fetch, because smoothing needs the readings _before_ a window in order to enter it warm — a range-scoped fetch would restart the trend at whatever the first day in view happened to read. Above 90 points a chart switches to weekly averages and says so.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| FR-45 | A goal may carry an optional **goal weight** (`calorie_profiles.goal_target_weight_kg`), set from the Hub profile form or `calories_update_profile`. With one, the Progress card reports the run as a share of the whole journey (a bar, "X of Y kg to goal", "Z to go", a percentage), and estimates a finish date from the rate actually being achieved alongside the date the plan implied — the gap between the two is what says how much a slower rate is costing. The estimate is shown as a month and day inside 60 days and a month and year beyond, because it divides a noisy achieved rate into a distance and a to-the-day figure would overstate its precision; it is withheld entirely when the weight is moving away from the target or the arithmetic lands more than ten years out. A target on the wrong side of the baseline for the goal's direction is ignored rather than drawn. Without a goal weight the card behaves exactly as before, showing the rate only. The card also reports the last seven days' trend movement separately from the run total, so the week is visible as context without being the verdict. |
| FR-46 | Every decimal field on the Hub profile form uses `step="any"`, constraining range but never granularity. A fixed `step` made a stored value that was not a multiple of it (a 0.75 kg/week rate, which `calories_update_profile` accepts and which is a common choice; a height of 181.5 cm; a weight of 90.25 kg) fail _native_ constraint validation, and the browser then blocks submission before React's handler runs — no error, no request, the entire profile form silently unsaveable with no indication why. The form must always be able to save back what the rest of the system stored.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| FR-47 | Both weight charts plot against a **time-scaled x-axis** whose domain is the selected range, not the extent of the data. A categorical axis (one evenly-spaced slot per weigh-in) made time not to scale — a month with nothing logged drew the same width as a single day, so the trend's visible slope was meaningless — and it shrank the axis to whatever dates had data, so widening the range over a gap appeared to do nothing at all. With a real time axis, gaps render proportionally and an empty stretch is visibly empty. Where seven or more days at the start of the range hold no weigh-in, both cards say so explicitly ("No weigh-ins in the first 28 days of this range"), because a range that legitimately has little to show must not be indistinguishable from a broken control.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| FR-48 | The profile is readable as a **tool** (`calories_get_profile`), not only as the `calories://profile` resource. Resources are optional in the MCP spec and several clients never load them, which left those clients able to write the profile (`calories_update_profile`) but not read it back — so the assistant either planned against targets it could not see or had to write to discover them. The tool takes no arguments and returns the same payload as the resource: the profile, the calculated targets (`tdee`, `goalCalories`, `minCalories`, `maxCalories`), and the latest reading of every measurement type. The resource stays for clients that do support it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| FR-49 | Field guidance on the Hub profile form lives behind an **info icon** beside the field's label, not in its placeholder. A placeholder is the wrong home for an explanation: it disappears at the first keystroke, is clipped to the width of the input, and is styled like a value rather than like help. Goal weight was the worst case — its placeholder read `Optional \u2014 unlocks progress and an estimated finish date`, which was both far wider than the field could show and literally broken, the escape having been written into the source as six characters rather than an em dash. Placeholders are now reserved for a short example value (`e.g. 175`) or the single word `Optional`, and the explanation — what the field unlocks, what happens when it is left blank, what convention it follows — is reached by tapping the icon. Weight, gym day calorie bonus, training time, rate, goal weight, and min/max calories per day each carry one.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| FR-50 | Logging a meal eaten before is a single tap. The add-meal modal opens with a "Log again" row of the user's recently logged meals (`GET /api/calories/meals/recent`, backed by `getRecentMealSuggestions`), deduplicated by description compared case-insensitively and ordered newest first. Picking one fills the description, meal type and the macros that dish carried the last time it was logged, all still editable before saving; notes are not carried over, since a note describes one occasion rather than the dish. Suggestions whose last log used the currently selected meal type are listed first, but none are hidden — the row's contents stay stable as the meal type changes. The row appears only when adding, never when editing an existing meal, and renders nothing for a user with no history. |

| TR-06 | Measurement types are defined in `packages/shared/src/constants/measurements.ts` as a constant list of `{ key, label, unit }` objects (e.g. `weight`/kg, `height`/cm, `body_fat`/%), consumed by Hub and MCP services.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| TR-07 | `calories_get_profile` and TDEE-dependent summary tools must call `getLatestMeasurementsPerType(userId)` and pass the latest `height`/`weight` values to `calculateTDEE`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| TR-08 | Date defaults for calorie daily/history flows must use the authenticated user's stored timezone when available (falling back to server-local behavior only when timezone is unset/invalid).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| TR-09 | Weekly-menu storage uses four tables: `weekly_menus` (user + week_start), `weekly_menu_meals` (unique slot constraint `uq_weekly_menu_meal_slot`), `weekly_menu_day_logs` (logged-slot markers, unique per slot), and `weekly_menu_shopping_items` (unique per menu + text). Child tables cascade on menu delete.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| TR-10 | All weekly-menu queries live in `packages/shared/src/services/calories/weekly-menu.ts` and `shopping-list.ts`. Menu ownership checks go through `hasAccessToMenu` (1s promise cache); every code path that deletes a menu (delete, replace-on-create, delete-all) must evict the cache entry to avoid FK violations from stale grants.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| TR-11 | Hub route contracts for the feature are Zod schemas in `packages/hub/src/app/api/calories/menu/menu.schemas.ts`, shared by the routes (`route({ body, response })`) and the client (`apiFetch` `bodySchema`/`responseSchema`). The duplicate-slot rule is `hasDuplicateMealSlot` from `@my-hub/shared/utils`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| TR-12 | Meal ingredients are a nullable `text[]` column on `weekly_menu_meals` — deliberately unstructured (quantities live inside each string), not a normalised recipe model. Every write path normalises through one helper in `weekly-menu.ts`: trim, drop blanks, drop case-insensitive duplicates, and collapse an empty result to `null` so "no ingredients" has a single representation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| TR-13 | `rowToProfile` in `packages/mcp-server/src/calories/models/profile.ts` is the single read surface for the calorie profile — it feeds the `calories://profile` resource and the `calories_update_profile` response. Every profile column must reach it except internal keys (`id`, `userId`), `createdAt`, and `automationApiKey` (a secret). A unit test asserts exactly that against a fully-populated row, so a column added to the schema cannot silently fail to reach the assistant — which had already happened to `gymDayCalorieBonus` and the three macro targets.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| TR-14 | `getDailySummariesForRange(userId, start, end)` in `packages/shared/src/services/calories/day-summaries.ts` returns zero-filled per-day `{date, kcal, protein, carbs, fat, mealCount}` for an arbitrary date range, built on `getMealsForDateRange`. `GET /api/calories/calendar?month=YYYY-MM` (`packages/hub/src/app/api/calories/calendar/route.ts`, schemas in the sibling `calendar.schemas.ts`) layers `dayCalorieTargets` on top per day to add `target`/`min`/`isGymDay`, so a day's ceiling matches Progress and the weekly menu rather than being re-derived a third way.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| TR-15 | The weight-goal maths lives in `packages/shared/src/utils/weight-goal.ts` (pure, no DB or clock): `dailyGoalDeltaKg`, `resolveGoalAnchor`, `projectedWeightOn`, `buildWeightTrend`, `trendRateKgPerWeek`, `isAheadOfGoal`, `daysBetweenDateStr`. It is in `shared` rather than hub so the Hub cards and the weekly/monthly email reports can judge a goal the same way instead of each re-deriving it. Hub-side chart shaping (`buildGoalProgressView`, `averageWeeklyWeights`) stays in `packages/hub/src/app/calories/calories.utils.ts`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| TR-16 | `buildProfileSnapshot(userId)` in `packages/mcp-server/src/calories/models/profile.ts` is the single builder behind both profile read surfaces — the `calories://profile` resource and the `calories_get_profile` tool call it and return its result unchanged, so the two cannot drift. It composes `rowToProfile` (TR-13) with `getLatestMeasurementsPerType` and `profileToTargets`, mirroring how `buildDailySummary` backs both `calories://today` and `calories_get_daily_summary`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| TR-17 | The info affordance is the shared `FieldInfo` component (`packages/hub/src/components/FieldInfo.tsx`), reached by passing `info` to `Field`. Two details are load-bearing. Its trigger is a `<span role="button">` rather than a `<button>`, because `Field` renders a `<label>` wrapping its control and a `<label>` takes its labeled control from the first _labelable_ descendant — a real button placed before the input would silently steal the association and leave the input with no accessible name; the click is also `preventDefault`ed, since label activation runs as the click's default action and would otherwise focus (or, on a checkbox, toggle) the field. Its panel is portaled to `document.body` and positioned `fixed`, because the cards these forms sit in are `overflow-hidden` and would clip an absolutely-positioned one; it is clamped into the viewport and flips above the trigger when there is more room there. Opening is click/tap only, never hover, which has no touch equivalent. |
